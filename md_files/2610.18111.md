abstract: Although magnetic reconnection in coronal current sheets (CSs) is a fundamental
  energy release mechanism in the solar corona, the role of guide-fields in governing
  its formation and self-consistent evolution is not fully understood. Through 2.5D
  resistive magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) simulations, we investigate how guide-fields
  affect the formation and evolution of a localized reconnecting coronal CS. Strong
  guide-fields prevent oppositely directed field lines from approaching sufficiently
  to form a CS, delaying and suppressing its subsequent evolution. The reconnection
  rate exhibits a rising phase resembling a power-law, with exponent $δ_1$ decreasing
  with rising guide-fields, indicating slower temporal growth of the reconnection
  rate. The aspect ratio of the CS follows a similar power-law trend with the exponent
  $α_1$ decreasing with rising guide-fields. A subsequent slower growth phase of the
  reconnection rate shows a power-law exponent $δ_2$ that increases with rising guide-fields,
  as does the corresponding aspect-ratio exponent $α_2$ . As the CS evolves, the latter
  phase is achieved due to a sharp decremental transition of the guide-field within
  the CS after a time when the increasing inflows are inefficiently resisted by the
  guide-field itself. In the reconnection regime during prior phases, the power-law
  exponents of the maximum temperature and the outflow velocities also decrease with
  rising guide-fields demonstrating that stronger guide-fields suppress the growth
  of these indicators also. This study suggests that a self-consistently formed CS
  displays phases of efficient and inefficient quenching of the reconnection mechanism,
  promoted by non-zero guide-fields. We also quantify this behavior for indicators
  of the reconnection process and discuss their applicability to observations of apparent
  CSs and probing background coronal conditions.
