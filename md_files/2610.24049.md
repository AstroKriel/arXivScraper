abstract: Ellerman bombs (EBs) are compact, transient brightenings observed in the
  wings of Balmer lines and are believed to result from magnetic reconnection in the
  lower solar atmosphere. However, their signatures have rarely been observed simultaneously
  in multiple spectral lines sensitive to the lower atmosphere. We present novel high-resolution
  multiline spectropolarimetric observations of EBs obtained with the Visible Spectro-Polarimeter
  (ViSP) on the Daniel K. Inouye Solar Telescope (DKIST). Simultaneous observations
  reveal remarkable H $β$ wing enhancements extending up to $\sim$ 4~Å from the line
  core and intensity enhancements in the \ion{Na}{1}~D1 and \ion{Fe}{1}~6302 lines.
  Using multiline non-LTE inversion of the \ion{Na}{1} and \ion{Fe}{1} lines, we infer
  localized photospheric heating of up to $ΔT\sim$ 800~K, accompanied by weak upflows
  and downflows at EB locations. Comparing with synthetic observables from Bifrost
  radiative-MHD simulations, we find that synthetic spectra reproduce the observed
  H $β$ and \ion{Na}{1}~D wing enhancements and yield atmospheric stratification broadly
  consistent with those inferred from the non-LTE inversion. This study provides strong
  evidence that EBs are driven by photospheric magnetic reconnection below the temperature-minimum
  region, and places new constraints on the realistic EB models and small-scale energy
  release in the lower solar atmosphere.
