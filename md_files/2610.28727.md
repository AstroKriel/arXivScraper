abstract: Star formation is a hierarchical, clustered process in turbulent giant molecular
  clouds (GMCs), where close stellar encounters can strongly affect cluster evolution
  and feedback. However, gravitational softening in many hydrodynamic simulations
  of GMCs limits the treatment of these encounters. We present Arepo-CD (Collisional
  Dynamics), a framework that couples star-by-star collisional $N$ -body dynamics
  to the moving-mesh magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) code Arepo. Star-star interactions
  use unsoftened gravity, while interactions involving gas or collisionless components
  remain softened. Compact stellar subsystems are identified on the fly and evolved
  with a fourth-order forward symplectic integrator and slow-down algorithmic regularization
  coupled to the native Arepo timestep hierarchy. Benchmark tests against the collisional
  $N$ -body code PeTar show good agreement in cluster core collapse and subsequent
  expansion, radial density profiles, and stellar velocity distributions. Coupled
  to the Rigel star formation and feedback model, Arepo-CD follows cluster formation
  and early evolution in radiation MHD simulations of a $10^5~M_\odot$ turbulent molecular
  cloud. In the fiducial GMC comparison, the collisional and softened-gravity runs
  yield similar star formation histories and integrated star formation efficiencies.
  Nevertheless, close gravitational encounters during the embedded phase generate
  a substantial runaway population, with a velocity tail extending beyond 100 km/s.
  Ejected massive stars redistribute radiative feedback into the surrounding lower-density
  medium and produce a more extended distribution of heated gas. Arepo-CD provides
  a practical and computationally efficient framework for studying collisional stellar
  dynamics and its coupling to gas evolution and feedback during star cluster formation
  in realistic galactic environments.
