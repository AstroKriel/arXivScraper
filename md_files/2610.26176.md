abstract: We investigate the impact of turbulence and magnetic field on the formation
  of unipolar outflows in the gravitational collapse of isolated low-mass cloud cores
  using three-dimensional non-ideal magnetohydrodynamics simulations. In the models
  explored in this study, we find that unipolar outflows are formed in cloud cores
  with the super-Alfvénic turbulence of $M_{\rm{A}}>1$ , where $M_{\rm{A}}$ is the
  Alfvén Mach number of the initial cloud core. In contrast, bipolar outflows are
  formed in cloud cores with $M_{\rm{A}}\lesssim1$ . Our results suggest that the
  relative strength of the turbulence to the magnetic field in the initial cloud core
  plays a key role in the formation of unipolar outflows. We also find that no outflow
  is formed in cloud cores with the strong magnetic field of the dimensionless mass-to-flux
  ratio $μ=2$ . The outflow morphologies can be used as tracers to indirectly estimate
  the relative strengths of the turbulence and magnetic field of the parent cloud
  cores.
