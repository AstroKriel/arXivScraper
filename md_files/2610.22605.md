abstract: In this paper, we describe a novel technique for inferring electron density
  in laser plasmas from proton-radiography data. Proton radiography is primarily a
  diagnostic for imaging electromagnetic field structures in such plasmas. In the
  presence of strong field gradients, `caustic features arise and are broadened by
  the process of collisional scattering between the imaging protons and the plasma.
  This effect has a well defined density dependence, allowing for a robust measurement
  of electron number density. We validate the method on data from the turbulent-dynamo
  experiments performed at the Omega laser facility (with electron densities in the
  range of ${\sim}10^{20}\,\mathrm{cm^{-3}}$ ), achieving good agreement with values
  obtained from Thomson scattering, x-ray imaging and simulations. This research enables
  one of the most robust techniques for measuring electromagnetic fields on laser-plasma
  experiments to simultaneously provide density measurements along the same line of
  sight, improving our ability to diagnose such plasmas.
