abstract: OpenAI reports a forced Navier-Stokes flow that becomes singular in finite
  time, raising the question of its physical consequences. Magnetic reconnection offers
  a test through the splitting and merging of flux structures. We use resistive magnetohydrodynamics
  to simulate a magnetic ring driven by an imposed, regularized flow that reproduces
  the geometry and scaling of the collapsing core. At the nominal singular time, we
  remove the imposed drive and allow the system to evolve freely. Reconnections before
  and after release split the ring into two or more daughter rings through bifurcations
  of the poloidal flux function, with Lorentz feedback selecting the bifurcation type.
  Unequal winding by the swirl above and below the midplane produces asymmetric toroidal
  magnetic stresses, unfolding the kinematic pitchfork-like transition into a fold.
  For single-island splits, the cusp normal form predicts the asymmetry of the daughter
  rings without adjustable parameters. Three-dimensional simulations retain the splits
  and merger, confirming that approach to hydrodynamic blow-up can trigger magnetic
  restructuring.
