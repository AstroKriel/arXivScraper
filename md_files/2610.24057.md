abstract: The one-point distribution of the magnetic field direction in the young
  solar wind is found to be a superposition of two populations rather than a single
  exponential. Using Parker Solar Probe (PSP) Encounter 1 magnetometer data, we show
  that the distribution of the pitch-angle cosine mu(t) = bhat(t) . bhat_0 is well
  described by a two-component von Mises-Fisher (vMF) mixture... a broad, weakly confined
  component of concentration kappa_1 ~ 3.6 that accounts for the large-amplitude rotations
  and switchback tail, and a field-aligned component that reduces, in the small-angle
  limit, to a Gaussian in the polar angle theta of width sigma ~ 15 degrees (concentration
  kappa_2 ~ 14). The concentration of the field-aligned component is not a free parameter...
  it is set by the level of the transverse Alfvenic fluctuation through kappa_2 =
  2<|B|^2>/<delta B_perp^2> = 2/<1-mu^2> ~ 16.2, in agreement with the fitted value
  to within ~12%. The transverse fluctuations are weakly anisotropic (<delta B_1^2>/<delta
  B_2^2> ~ 1.1), so the core is a weakly anisotropic Bingham distribution rather than
  a pure vMF. The broad component is exponential in mu, not in theta, confirming that
  switchbacks saturate toward mu = -1 rather than decaying away, consistent with the
  drift-diffusion framework of Bian et al. (2024, ApJ, 960, L15). This physics-based
  two-population framework connects macroscopic fluid parameters directly to those
  describing the directional statistics, offering a powerful diagnostic for probing
  the structural evolution of MHD turbulence and switchbacks in the inner heliosphere.
