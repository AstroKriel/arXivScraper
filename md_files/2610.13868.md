abstract: Nucleosynthesis simulations of the r-process require neutron capture rates
  for neutron-rich nuclei, many of which lie beyond experimental reach. Predicting
  these rates relies on theoretical calculations of the low-energy electric dipole
  (E1) strength near the neutron threshold. We present the first ab initio computation
  of the E1 strength along the nickel isotopic chain, for $^{58}$ Ni and the even-even
  isotopes $^{68-80}$ Ni. The E1 strength is obtained with the Lorentz integral transform
  coupled-cluster (LIT-CC) method, extended to axially symmetric reference states
  to reach open-shell nuclei, using two- and three-nucleon interactions from chiral
  effective field theory. The approach is benchmarked against the measured photoabsorption
  cross section of $^{58}$ Ni and the E1 strength of $^{68}$ Ni. Along the chain we
  find low-lying E1 strength above the neutron threshold that grows with neutron number.
  Including it in the reaction code TALYS enhances the neutron capture rates forming
  $^{68-78}$ Ni between 3 and 10 GK, with an effect that increases with temperature
  and neutron number and an uncertainty dominated by the inversion of the integral
  transform. For $^{80}$ Ni, where strong pygmy dipole strength lies just above threshold,
  the enhancement sets in already around 0.5 GK. Together, these results open the
  way to ab initio input for neutron capture rates along isotopic chains far from
  stability.
