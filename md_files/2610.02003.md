abstract: We show that a two-dimensional chiral fluid supports an antisymmetric contribution
  to the pressure, and determine its microscopic expression. Its origin lies outside
  the standard kinetic and collisional channels of the normal pressure tensor, stemming
  instead from the average of a parity-breaking pseudo-scalar. This chiral pressure
  is set by the rotational (vortex) viscosity of micropolar hydrodynamics, whose kinetic
  value for rough disks we compute here, to Gaussian order. Crucially, both the chiral
  pressure and the rotational viscosity survive at zeroth order in the gradients,
  meaning they persist even in the fully homogeneous fluid -- which we show is not
  only well defined but steady (the Quiescent Chiral State, QCS). Since this averaged
  pseudo-scalar does not vanish in a chiral fluid, any expansion of its distribution
  function must contain terms in this pseudo-scalar variable.
