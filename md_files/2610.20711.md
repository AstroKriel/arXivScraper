abstract: Magnetic fields are dynamically important across many astronomical environments,
  yet their origin, strength, and evolution over cosmic time remain poorly understood.
  Ordered magnetic fields are ubiquitous in the nearby Universe, as revealed by a
  range of observational techniques, but evidence for their existence at cosmological
  distances is scarce. Recent ALMA detections of polarised dust emission indicate
  that galactic-scale ordered magnetic fields, spanning up to kiloparsec scales, were
  already present in massive star-forming galaxies at redshifts up to $z\sim5.6$ .
  This white paper argues that the ALMA2040 concept -- with its nearly tenfold improvement
  in sensitivity and angular resolution, and full-polarisation capability extended
  to higher-frequency bands -- will open up the study of cosmic magnetism in the early
  Universe. We identify four science drivers... constraining the dynamos that generate
  galactic-scale ordered fields, measuring the strength of early magnetic fields,
  connecting magnetic fields to galaxy evolution across cosmic time, and constraining
  dust grain properties through the polarised spectral energy distribution. We summarise
  the corresponding requirements in angular resolution, spectral coverage, and sensitivity,
  and discuss synergies with next-generation telescopes.
