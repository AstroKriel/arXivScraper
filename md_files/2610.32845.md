abstract: The origin of the excess of GeV gamma-rays in the Milky Ways galactic center
  observed by Fermi-LAT has been an open question for nearly two decades. The two
  leading interpretations of this signal are that it is produced either by annihilating
  dark matter (DM), or arises from unresolved point sources, such as a novel population
  of millisecond pulsars. In both scenarios, uncertainties and limitations in the
  modeling of galactic diffuse gamma-ray emission, such as the steady-state approximation
  of cosmic ray (CR) propagation and assumption of an axisymmetric distribution of
  CRs, complicate the interpretation of the galactic center excess (GCE). Simulations
  with full magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) and CR transport can provide a new avenue for
  understanding the astrophysical phenomena that drive galactic diffuse gamma-ray
  emission. We utilize MHD simulations of Milky Way analogs with time-dependent, spectrally-resolved
  CR transport to reconstruct diffuse gamma-ray emission. Comparing instantaneous
  emission to a time-averaged baseline, we find residuals up to several times larger
  than the Fermi GCE, with a wide range of spectral shapes and spatial morphologies.
  At some simulation snapshots, the spectrum and morphology of the residuals resemble
  the Fermi GCE. Our results suggest that the Fermi GCE may be a manifestation of
  the look-elsewhere effect- a transient fluctuation in diffuse emission from stellar
  sources, observed at an inopportune moment- and motivate the development of new
  Fermi-LAT analysis templates that account for time-dependent CR transport.
