abstract: Context. Solar energetic particles (SEPs) in the heliosphere are produced
  by flaring processes on the Sun or shocks driven by coronal mass ejections (CMEs).
  Electrons, in particular, are regularly detected remotely as electromagnetic radiation
  (X-rays or radio emission), which they generate through various processes, as well
  as in situ by spacecraft monitoring the Sun and the heliosphere. Aims. We aim to
  combine remote-sensing and in situ observations of an energetic solar eruption to
  link energetic processes in the solar corona to energetic particles observed in
  situ in the heliosphere. This will be done using the tools developed in the Horizon
  Europe SOLER project. Methods. Here, we investigate a solar energetic eruption at
  the Sun together with its associated signatures of accelerated electrons observed
  at radio and X-ray wavelengths and combine these remote observations to in situ
  particles observed by spacecraft on 10 March 2024. These observations are combined
  with a magneto-hydrodynamic (MHD) model of the solar corona in order to make the
  remote to in situ connections. Results. We observe a high-energy SEP event at multiple
  spacecraft (>25 MeV protons and 1 MeV electrons), despite the event being associated
  with a slow white-light CME. Remote observations of the Sun show that the event
  was associated with a fast large scale coronal wave (1400 km/s) and also multiple
  regions where electrons are accelerated around this large scale wave observed as
  type II radio bursts. In addition, we observe a strong coronal dimming away from
  the main flaring region, providing evidence of field lines getting opened to interplanetary
  space, dislocated from the flare site.
