abstract: Context. Short-term millimeter-wavelength variability in Class II young
  stellar objects (YSOs) has been considered minimal, often dismissed as very rare
  in ALMA-based studies on short timescales. However, recent findings suggest that
  variability on sub-day timescales is significant. Aims. This study aims to quantify
  short timescale (~1 hour to ~3 days) millimeter flux variability in unresolved Class
  II YSO disks observed as part of the SODA survey, leveraging its large dataset to
  assess the statistical significance of such variability across the LDN 1641 and
  LDN 1647 regions in Orion. Methods. We analyze ALMA Band 6 observations of 873 YSOs
  obtained over multiple epochs using a custom flux extraction pipeline to separate
  individual execution blocks. By computing variability factors and statistical deviations
  from the mean flux, we identify sources exhibiting significant variability, accounting
  for noise and calibrator stability. Results. Even with conservative selection criteria,
  we identify 20 variable sources within the sample (~5.3% of all detections). The
  maximum of the measured variability factor is >=7 over about one hour. More liberal
  selection criteria yield up to 60 sources that could be called potentially variable
  (~15.9%). Conclusions. The number of variable sources detected in our sample indicates
  physical processes operating on time scales of hours, likely associated with magnetic
  reconnection and/or synchrotron radiation or strong cases of magnetic accretion
  changes. These detections show the need to reconsider typical stability assumptions
  in single-epoch observations and calls for follow-up studies in star-forming regions
  with ALMA. Masses derived from unresolved measurements could potentially be biased
  by this sort of variability.
