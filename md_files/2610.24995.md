abstract: Parker Solar Probe (PSP) observed a coronal mass ejection (CME)-driven shock
  at 0.07 au on 2022 September 5, and the same CME-driven interplanetary shock was
  subsequently observed by Solar Orbiter (SolO) at 0.7 au on 2022 September 6. Such
  a large difference in heliocentric distance between the two shock events enables
  our comparative study of particle acceleration in near-Sun and far-Sun regions.
  Using a backward-in-time test-particle approach in a slab-2D turbulent magnetic
  field model, we perform particle acceleration simulations for the 0.07 au PSP shock
  and 0.7 au SolO shock, covering the energy ranges measured by PSP/EPI-Lo (0.0697-8.06
  MeV) and SolO/EPD (0.0495-5.83 MeV). With an initial kappa = 3 kappa distribution,
  the particle fluxes at the shock passage are successfully reproduced using effective
  local shock angles of 70 degrees for the PSP shock and 80 degrees for the SolO shock.
  Both the observed and simulated energy spectra exhibit a double power-law form.
  The 0.07 au PSP spectrum softens above 0.75 MeV, while the 0.7 au SolO spectrum
  hardens slightly above 0.51 MeV, revealing distinct near-Sun and far-Sun acceleration
  environments. Compared with the observations, the simulations yield harder energy
  spectra with higher break energies of 2 MeV at 0.07 au and 3 MeV at 0.7 au, indicating
  efficient particle acceleration under the initial kappa = 3 distribution. Both the
  observed and simulated spectral indices are harder than the standard diffusive shock
  acceleration (DSA) prediction, likely arising from non-local acceleration at the
  large-angle quasi-perpendicular shocks, efficient injection of pre-accelerated suprathermal
  particles, and the finite shock lifetime effect.
