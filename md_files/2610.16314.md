abstract: Context... Solar magnetism exhibits long-term variations on timescales of
  decades to centuries. Aims... The main aim is to study how the supercriticality
  of the dynamo affects the variability of magnetic fields on timescales relevant
  to the Sun. Methods... Three-dimensional rotating magnetohydrodynamic simulations
  in a spherical wedge geometry are used to produce self-consistent convection-driven
  dynamos. The supercriticality of the dynamo is controlled by varying the magnetic
  diffusivity. Results... The main result is that long-term variability of the dynamo
  increases with decreasing supercriticality of the dynamo and that cases nearest
  to the onset of dynamo action show extended grand minima-like periods of low activity.
  The simulations fall in three classes where the least supercritical cases show the
  largest variations of magnetic activity and where the flow including differential
  rotation is almost unaffected by the magnetic field. In the cases with the highest
  supercriticality the magnetic field is permanently strong and differential rotation
  permanently quenched, both having relatively little temporal variation. In an intermediate
  regime both the magnetic fields and differential rotation exhibit strong anticorrelated
  fluctuations. Conclusions... The occurrence of grand minima in the Sun has been
  conjectured to be due to a weakly supercritical dynamo. The current results support
  this idea and suggest that the absence of grand minima in earlier global dynamo
  simulations is because their large-scale dynamos were too supercritical. Furthermore,
  the current results suggest that the effect of the magnetic field on differential
  rotation is negligible if the average rms-field is less than about 2~kG.
