abstract: Reconnection-based models of coronal heating predict that magnetic complexity---non-potential
  configurations, currents, shear---should enhance coronal emission independently
  of total magnetic flux. Previous work, however, found no evidence that complexity
  contributes independently to coronal heating (Fisher et al. 1998). We revisit this
  question using SDO/HMI vector magnetograms and SDO/AIA EUV imaging. HMI Space-weather
  Active Region Patches (SHARPs) provide 16 magnetic parameters characterizing field
  strength, gradients, currents, helicity, and non-potentiality. We measure spatially-integrated
  transition region and coronal emission using AIA 304A, 193A, 94A, and isolated Fe
  XVIII emission for 137 active regions, sampling plasma at ~0.09MK, ~1.4MK, and ~7.1MK.
  Partial correlation analysis isolates the contribution of each complexity parameter
  after controlling for total unsigned vertical magnetic flux. Unsigned flux (Phi_M)
  explains 72--82\% of variance in emission, following power laws (I ~ Phi_M^alpha,
  where alpha=0.61-1.19) consistent with prior soft X-ray results. Partial correlations
  reveal weak positive contributions (r = 0.2-0.3) to transition region (304A) emission
  from some vertical current and field parameters, but little evidence that other
  active-region-averaged measures of magnetic complexity enhance coronal emission.
  The exception is total photospheric magnetic free energy density (rho_tot), which
  shows a positive partial correlation (r = 0.49, p < 0.001) with Fe XVIII emission,
  but negative, or non-existent, partial correlations with other AIA filters. These
  results suggest quasi-steady coronal heating scales with magnetic flux, while rho_tot
  may contribute to high temperature coronal heating. At lower temperatures, regions
  with more stored energy than expected for their flux produce less EUV emission than
  expected.
