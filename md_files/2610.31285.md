abstract: Coronal jets are transient phenomena that occur throughout the solar atmosphere,
  often triggered by the activation of mini-filaments. Jets are an important field
  of study, as they are believed to contribute to the mass supply of the solar wind.
  We study a small-scale solar jet occurring in a low-latitude coronal hole, focusing
  on traces of the jet eruption to the lower atmosphere, utilizing chromospheric and
  transition region diagnostics. We also investigate the possible mechanisms and dynamic
  conditions leading to its onset. We analyze ultraviolet spectra from the Interface
  Region Imaging Spectrograph to investigate chromospheric dynamics and their connection
  to the transition region, using diagnostics of the Mg II k 2796.35 Å and Si IV 1393.75
  Å lines, together with non-LTE inversions. We complement this analysis with multiwavelength
  imaging and magnetograms from the Solar Dynamics Observatory, as well as magnetic
  field modeling. The analysis of Mg II k and Si IV parameters reveals a coupling
  between chromospheric and transition region plasma dynamics. Profiles on the jet
  spire present deeper central reversals, larger peak separations and higher transition
  region Doppler velocities. The jet initiation is associated with a mini-filament-like
  structure, preceded by dynamic variations in the photospheric magnetic field. We
  find single-peaked Mg II k profiles at one of its footpoints, possibly indicating
  plasma drainage. The presence of a null point and a quasi-separatrix layer suggests
  that reconnection can play a role in the mini-filament-like structure destabilization
  and jet eruption. This study suggests that the signature and possibly the origin
  of coronal jets can be traced down to the chromosphere. The detailed analysis of
  the Mg II k profiles at the jet spire and the mini-filament-like structure may provide
  valuable observational constraints for future MHD simulations.
