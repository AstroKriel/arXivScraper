abstract: We present numerical magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) simulations of the interstellar
  medium in gravity-stratified boxes with the Piernik MHD code. The system contains
  thermal gas, spectrally resolved cosmic-ray (CR) nuclei within the CRESP algorithm,
  and spectrally unresolved CR protons. CR protons drive gas outflows and undergo
  Coulomb and hadronic losses. CR nuclei undergo Coulomb losses. We also include the
  spallation of primary Carbon by thermal gas atoms, thereby decaying Car- bon into
  secondary stable Boron isotopes. We use the resistive-MHD approximation as a subgrid
  model for unresolved turbulent magnetic dissipation in current sheets. We perform
  a parameter study of the magnetic diffusivity η. The Boron-to-Carbon ratio B/C and
  hadronic γ-ray luminosity density are computed as diagnostics. We find that non-zero
  magnetic diffusivity modifies the magnetic topology, leading to a more horizontal
  field in the halo, thereby accumulating CRs in the disk region and signifi- cantly
  increasing the CR escape time. As a consequence, the local turbulent conditions
  and turbulent reconnection in the interstellar medium should be considered when
  interpreting B/C and hadronic γ-ray luminosity observed at Earth. Our results suggest
  that the escape time and diffusion coefficient of CRs deduced from observational
  data in the vicinity of the Solar System cannot be extrapolated to the whole Galaxy.
