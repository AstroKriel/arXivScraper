abstract: It has recently been demonstrated that the occurrence of extreme solar events
  is governed by a phase-stable period close to 1.723 years (629.29 days). According
  to our previously developed tidally-synchronized solar dynamo model, this is one
  of the periods resulting from two-planet spring tides exerted by the tidally dominant
  planets Venus, Earth and Jupiter, which trigger magneto-Rossby waves in the solar
  tachocline. Overall, the squared sum of these three waves incorporates nine periods
  ranging from 59 to 629 days. Using a standard Rayleigh test, we demonstrate that
  all nine of these periods can be identified in the data on extreme solar events.
  For the latter, we use a combination of 72 ground-level enhancement (GLE) events
  with various X-flare data, the number of which increases when the threshold is successively
  lowered from X10 to X1. To identify the spectral peaks, it is necessary to analyze
  the sidebands that emerge due to the modulation with the longer-term periods such
  as the 11-year Schwabe cycle and the third harmonics of the Hale cycle. In this
  framework, the seminal Rieger period of 154 days is re-interpreted as the lower
  sideband of 146 days which is the quarter of the synodic period of Venus and Earth.
