abstract: Magnetar bursts are commonly attributed to magnetic energy release, yet
  the physically relevant dissipation unit has remained uncertain. Here we argue that
  this unit is not the burst as conventionally defined, but the short substructures
  within bursts, namely magnetar X-ray flares (MXFs). Using 789 MXFs from SGR~J1935+2154,
  we show that their waiting times separate into two components, interpreted as reflecting
  clustered intra-episode events and longer inter-episode intervals, that their proxy
  emission measure and temperature exhibit distinct rising and declining trends, and
  that their energy release scale (ERS) proxy distribution has a high-end slope in
  the same statistical regime as solar and stellar flares. Combined with the previously
  measured MXF duration slope, these results identify MXFs as a three-dimensional
  fractal-diffusive self-organized critical (FD-SOC) system with $β_{\rm diff}\simeq2$
  , implying reconnection avalanches that expand linearly through an active magnetosphere.
  We infer that magnetar active episodes are global unloading states assembled from
  many elementary flares. Magnetar X-ray flares are therefore the physically relevant
  units for magnetar outburst statistics and for connecting magnetar high-energy activity
  to other impulsive phenomena.
