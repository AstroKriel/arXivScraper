abstract: We investigate an X9.0 class flare (SOL2024-10-03T12...08) and associated
  quasi-periodic pulsations (QPPs) in NOAA active region (AR) 13842 using observations
  from the Solar Dynamics Observatory (SDO), Aditya-L1 and Solar Orbiter (SO). To
  explore the role of magnetic configuration in the flare, we employ non-force-free-field
  extrapolation technique for reconstructing a sequence of the coronal magnetic field
  throughout the flare. The observations reveal the emergence of intricate flare brightenings
  and ribbons. QPPs are observed with periods of $\approx$ 2 and $\approx$ 6 minutes
  in EUV, $\approx$ 7 minutes in UV, and $\approx$ 1 minute in X-rays. The extrapolation
  documents the presence of a flux rope, a sheared arcade, and a hyperbolic flux tube
  (HFT) enveloping the rope and the arcade. The sequence of the extrapolation illustrates
  an increase in the twist of the rope as it ascends towards a torus-unstable region,
  thereby rendering the rope a viable candidate for eruption. The sheared arcade appears
  to generate electric current through its foot-points motion, which explains the
  sigmoidal brightening observed at the flare onset. The motion also seems to drive
  the non-parallel field lines closer together, heightening gradient in the magnetic
  field beneath the rope. Consequently, recurrent magnetic reconnection is anticipated
  within the sheared arcade --- explaining the ascent of the rope with its twist increment.
  The formation of parallel flare ribbons is attributed to these reconnections. Furthermore,
  reconnection at the HFT is proposed to lead the remote and quasi-circular ribbons.
  Crucially, repeated reconnections inside the arcade as well as at the HFT are speculated
  to cause the QPPs.
