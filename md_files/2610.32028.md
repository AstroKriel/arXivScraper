abstract: In this paper, we review the impact of a strong magnetic field on the neutral
  two-flavor color superconducting (2SC) phase of dense quark matter. Using a Nambu-Jona-Lasinio
  (NJL) model approach in the mean-field approximation, we first solve the gap equations
  for the diquark condensate $Δ$ and the electric chemical potential $μ_e$ under a
  uniform external magnetic field, and discuss the coupled evolution of the chiral
  and diquark order parameters. We then evaluate the field-dependent Meissner masses
  of the rotationally charged gluons for a wide range of densities and moderate diquark
  coupling strengths. Combining the `zero-mode problem` mechanism with the neutrality
  condition, we show that the gluon masses become tachyonic even at moderate magnetic-field
  values, signaling a chromomagnetic instability in density regions where the system
  was stable at zero field. This instability can be resolved through the restructuring
  of the ground state with the formation of a gluon vortex condensate. A remarkable
  consequence of the vortex configuration is the paramagnetic amplification of the
  in-medium magnetic field... seed fields of the order of $10^{15}$ G can be enhanced
  by up to two orders of magnitude, reaching $\sim 10^{17}$ G, precisely the range
  predicted for magnetar cores. We compare this microscopic mechanism with the conventional
  dynamo explanation and discuss how it offers a plausible explanation for the extremely
  high magnetic fields expected to be realized in magnetar interiors.
