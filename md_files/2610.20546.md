abstract: Parker Solar Probe (PSP) revealed the ubiquity of switchbacks (SBs), sharp,
  localised magnetic deflections in the nascent solar wind, renewing interest in their
  origin. One proposed mechanism involves solar jets... collimated, impulsive features
  driven by magnetic reconnection that launch Alfvénic torsional wavefronts into the
  heliosphere. We test whether realistic spacecraft sampling of a propagating solar
  jet produces in-situ signatures consistent with PSP observations. We also examine
  the 3D magnetic topology of the resulting structures and how deflection amplitudes
  evolve with the ambient plasma environment. We perform a 3D magnetohydrodynamic
  simulation of a solar jet in the ambient solar wind, embedding a virtual spacecraft
  trajectory based on PSPs actual 22nd-perihelion orbit to generate synthetic measurements.
  The simulation reproduces SB-like signatures with durations and profiles consistent
  with PSP observations, confirming earlier results from simplified trajectories.
  We visualise the fully 3D magnetic topology of a jet-driven SB region, identifying
  two field-line families... one with a single deflection and another with two sequential
  deflections along the same field line. Both are outward-propagating Alfvénic structures.
  Deflection amplitudes depend strongly on background plasma $β$ , increasing substantially
  in low- $β$ regions and evolving more moderately where $β\sim 1$ . These results
  show that solar jets can produce Alfvénic, SB-like structures along realistic spacecraft
  trajectories. Their topology is inherently 3D and structurally diverse, and their
  evolution depends on ambient plasma $β$ . Complete magnetic reversals would require
  longer residence times in low- $β$ environments. Our findings support a solar origin
  for a fraction of SBs and constrain interpretations of their underlying magnetic
  structure.
