abstract: Accurate prediction of the magnetic structure of interplanetary coronal
  mass ejections (ICMEs) remains a key challenge in space weather forecasting. Despite
  several efforts using global MHD modeling, it remains difficult to deduce a flux-rope
  solution that can accurately and reliably reproduce the magnetic structure of a
  CME. We present FROST (Flux Rope embedded in the Solar Magnetic Field), a new flux-rope
  model implementation within the European Heliospheric Forecast Information Asset
  (EUHFORIA), featuring an initially force-free toroidal flux rope embedded in the
  low-coronal magnetic field. FROST employs a novel embedding technique that self-consistently
  generates a draping magnetic field around the flux-rope, ensuring continuity of
  the normal magnetic field component at the CME boundary. The dynamics of the flux-rope
  in the low and middle corona is solved by a non-uniform advection constrained by
  the observed kinematics of the event. This results in a global, non-toroidal, stretched
  loop-like magnetic structure. At heliospheric distances, the evolution is modeled
  as an MHD process using EUHFORIA. We validate the FROST model implementation by
  simulating the CME event of 2013 April 11 and comparing the results with in situ
  magnetic field measurements at 1 au. The modeled magnetic field profiles of the
  flux-rope exhibit strong agreement with the observations, with correlation coefficients
  ranging from 0.64-0.93 for Bx, 0.92-0.95 for By, and 0.82-0.87 for Bz component
  of the magnetic field. These results demonstrate the capability of the proposed
  flux-rope model to significantly improve ICME magnetic field prediction, highlighting
  its strong potential for advancing space-weather forecasting capabilities.
