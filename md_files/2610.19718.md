abstract: Flare ribbons mark the footpoints of newly reconnected magnetic loops and
  are commonly associated with strong plasma flows and enhanced non-thermal broadening
  of transition-region lines due to impulsive energy deposition. However, the mechanisms
  governing energy transport into flare ribbons remain uncertain, particularly under
  different magnetic field configurations. We investigate the energy deposition process
  at a flare ribbon near a sunspot by determining the physical nature of its small-scale
  downflows. We study small-scale downflows in a remote ribbon during the M7.7-class
  event on 30 September 2024, using coordinated observations from SDO, IRIS, and Solar
  Orbiter. We use stereoscopic imaging from Solar Orbiter and IRIS to detect plane-of-sky
  motions on small scales (200 km). We conduct spectroscopic analysis of IRIS Si IV
  profiles, complemented by C II and Mg II diagnostics. Apparent downflow speeds measured
  from Solar Orbiter imaging (limb view) are 85-130 km/s, while IRIS images (disk
  view) reveal plane-of-sky speeds of 70-85 km/s. In contrast, IRIS Si IV profiles
  at the brightest ribbon pixels are well approximated by a single Gaussian, with
  minimal Doppler shifts and unexpectedly small non-thermal widths (10 km/s). Peak
  Si IV emission also coincides with a deeper Mg II central reversal, while electron
  densities are one to two orders of magnitude higher than typical quiet-Sun values.
  The absence of significant line-of-sight velocities and non-thermal broadening differs
  from typical flare-ribbon behavior. Our results are consistent with a scenario in
  which plasma motions are guided by highly inclined (nearly horizontal) magnetic
  fields in chromospheric canopy and are not accompanied by strong turbulence or wave
  motions. This study suggests that flare ribbons can exhibit a wide diversity of
  lower-atmospheric responses and emphasizes the critical role of magnetic field geometry.
