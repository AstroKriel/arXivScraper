abstract: Supermassive black hole binaries (SMBHB) are expected at the center of galaxies
  as a result of galaxy mergers. The dynamics and evolution of SMBHBs shed light on
  the evolution and formation of galaxies, the gravitational wave background and the
  nature of gravity. Many methods have been proposed to identify sub-parsec SMBHBs
  via the detection of gravitational waves or the modulation of the emission from
  the surrounding gaseous environment. In a previous study, we proposed a new method
  of identifying SMBHBs in non-active galactic nuclei via gravitational lensing. The
  caustics, i.e. the source positions with a divergent magnification, exhibit a complex
  extended geometry which rotates with the binary period. Each caustic-crossing produces
  an extreme magnification by up to a factor of $10^{6}$ . In this work, we generalize
  the previous study and describe the rich phenomenology of binary-lens amplification
  of starlight (BLAST). We explore the electromagnetic transients produced when accounting
  for the relative proper motion of the source, either a single or a binary star,
  the eccentricity and precession of the SMBHB, and the relative inclination. Systems
  where a star rapidly drifts across the caustic produce a single- or double-peaked
  flare. In the opposite limit, a star with no relative proper motion produces quasi-periodic
  lensed starlight (QPLS), i.e., repeated peaks that track the systems orbital evolution.
  We determine the expected magnification and peak duration in different circumstances,
  and calculate the magnification curves and their Fourier transforms. This study
  may help construct search pipelines to identify SMBHBs in electromagnetic time-domain
  surveys.
