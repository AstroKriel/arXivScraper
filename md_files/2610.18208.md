abstract: The coalescence of binary neutron star systems offers unprecedented possibilities
  for multimessenger observations, including the detection of gravitational waves,
  gamma-ray bursts, kilonovae, and potentially fast radio bursts. For a detailed investigation
  of the latter, one requires a kinetic plasma model to describe the interacting magnetospheres
  of the stars. We present a three-dimensional particle-in-cell kinetic simulation
  of the combined magnetosphere of a binary neutron star in which the two stars physically
  orbit one another so that the orbital motion itself twists the magnetic loops. We
  find that the stellar magnetic-field intensity ratio controls the emergence of flares,
  while the stellar spins set the direction and modulation of the flare. We identify
  four spatially and dynamically distinct regions of strong electromagnetic energy
  release... (i) an expanding fast magnetosonic wave that steepens into a monster
  shock, (ii) a plasma flare being pushed by (iii) an expanding fireball and containing
  (iv) a reconnecting current sheet. The fast magnetosonic wave is a promising source
  of coherent radio emission. It is released when orbital motion cuts a closed plasma
  loop. The electric and magnetic wave amplitudes are similar and reach that of the
  background magnetic field. Our kinetic treatment yields particle spectra and plasma
  structures that can be used to assess coherent emission in kinetic-scale simulations.
  In conclusion, the stellar orbital motion constitutes a driving channel distinct
  from models with spin-driven magnetospheric twisting.
