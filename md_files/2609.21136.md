abstract: Radiation pressure and radiative cooling often play an important role in
  deciding the structure and dynamics of astrophysical objects. In this paper, we
  present a radiative MHD simulation of a circumbinary disk (CBD) around an equal-mass
  SMBBH. It employs an approximate thermodynamic model based on local thermodynamic
  equilibrium and diffusion cooling which captures how the internal energy evolves
  in an optically thick region. The disk reaches a quasi-steady state within $r=5a$
  whose mass distribution differs substantially from the one obtained from simulations
  without physical thermodynamics... the local maximum in the azimuthally-averaged
  surface density and the lump are completely erased; the time-averaged inner edge
  becomes more eccentric; and the vertical density distribution depends strongly on
  the radius. These changes are attributable to the enhancement of the magnetic fields
  caused by the cooling-driven vertical compression of the disk. Our result emphasizes
  that the basic properties of the inner CBD are sensitive to both radiation and magnetic
  physics.
