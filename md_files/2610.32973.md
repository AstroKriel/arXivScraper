abstract: While defect dynamics in condensed matter systems is widely studied and
  several methods are available to track the defect position over time, the formulation
  of dynamical models for the coupled evolution of both defect and background field
  quantities seems to be more challenging. Yet, the overall behavior of a system containing
  moving defects is strongly influenced by this defect-field interaction. Here, we
  propose a modeling framework for the conservative coupled evolution of the defect
  density and the background fields upon focusing on two relevant cases of study...
  the Gross-Pitaevskii theory of quantum fluids and the Ericksen-Leslie model of nematic
  liquid crystals. To achieve the coupled evolution, we combine the Euler-Poincaré
  variational setting of continuum dynamics with the gauge theory of topological defects.
  While the latter allows us to overcome certain ambiguities in definition of the
  defect density, the Euler-Poincaré method provides a unified modeling approach in
  the absence of defect reconnection.
