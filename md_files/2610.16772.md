abstract: Magnetic activity is a result of the interaction between rotation and dynamo
  processes in cool stars with outer convection zones. Since open cluster members
  share a common age, distance, and chemical composition, they provide an opportunity
  to study the magnetic activity of cool stars in a controlled astrophysical context.
  We used 120s cadence observations from the Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite
  (TESS) to study the flare activity of stars in three open clusters with different
  ages... the Pleiades (100 Myr), the Hyades (700 Myr) and Praesepe (700 Myr). We
  used the Ohio State Multi-Object Spectrograph (OSMOS) at MDM Observatory with the
  goal of identifying coronal mass ejections (CMEs) in the Pleiades and Praesepe.
  We investigated the flaring characteristics of the cluster members using the TESS
  light curves... we identified the flares, then divided the dataset into different
  categories based on the effective temperature and light curve amplitude. We calculated
  the flare energies and investigated the flare frequency distributions. We studied
  the dynamic spectra of the H $α$ line and its environment to search for indication
  of CMEs. The power-law indices of the flare frequency distributions obtained in
  our analysis span the 1.8-2.8 range. We found the highest flare rates in the Pleiades
  for all investigated spectral types. Our results show that the flare rate of M dwarfs
  is similar in the Pleiades and the Hyades while for K-type stars the flare rate
  in the Pleiades is an order of magnitude higher. Our analysis reveals that the flare
  rate in M stars changes with their effective temperature... the rate is highest
  for T $_{eff}$ $\sim$ 3200-3300 K -coinciding with the transition between partially
  and fully convective stars - with smooth decrease in flare rate towards cooler and
  hotter M dwarfs. We used the MDM/OSMOS spectra to place an upper limit on the CME
  rate.
