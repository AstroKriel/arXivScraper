abstract: Magnetic fields regulate accretion in T Tauri stars (TTSs) and evolve as
  radiative cores emerge during pre-main-sequence contraction. Stars above ~0.35-0.5
  Msun transition from fully convective to partially convective interiors, a change
  associated with increasingly complex magnetic topologies and evolving dynamo behaviour.
  Because accretion flows are magnetically channelled, this evolution is expected
  to modify ultraviolet (UV) accretion diagnostics, although observational evidence
  has remained limited. We identify a transition in the UV continuum properties of
  TTSs using archival International Ultraviolet Explorer observations, complemented
  by recent spectra from the Hubble Space Telescope ULLYSES survey. An empirical UV
  photospheric sequence constructed from G-M main-sequence stars shows that late K-M
  TTSs broadly follow standard photosphere-plus-shock model predictions, whereas G-K5
  stars display systematically lower F270/F303 continuum flux ratios (flux at 270
  nm relative to 303 nm). Statistical analysis shows that transition models describe
  the observations significantly better than a single continuous trend, locating the
  transition near spectral type K5-K6, with its precise location limited by spectral-classification
  uncertainties, and consistent with the fully convective boundary. Comparison with
  photosphere-plus-shock models further shows that, while the late-type population
  is broadly encompassed by the parameter space sampled by the adopted models, the
  warmer stars extend systematically beyond it in this UV diagnostic. We interpret
  this transition as evidence for a change in magnetospheric accretion associated
  with the evolution of magnetic topology across the radiative-core boundary, resulting
  in a corresponding change in the emergent UV continuum.
