abstract: We study a long-duration, confined X2.0-class solar flare exhibiting three
  distinct episodes of HXR emission, along with an accompanying EUV late phase. A
  key highlight of this work is the novel observations from the high-resolution solar
  X-ray spectrometers SoLEXS and HEL1OS onboard Aditya-L1, providing insights into
  the evolution of thermal and non-thermal emissions. Simultaneous EUV observations
  from AIA/SDO in hot channels, combined with multi-band X-ray light curves, reveal
  a two-stage confined eruption of a large MFR, separated by $\sim$ 10~minutes. The
  first stage corresponds to an M4.6 flare, while the overall event reaches X2.0 on
  the GOES scale. During the first phase, magnetic reconnection beneath the large
  EUV hot channel causes plasma heating to $\sim$ 19~MK, with a non-thermal spectral
  index of $\sim$ 4.3. In the second phase, characterized by external reconnection
  and confinement of the MFR hot channel, the temperature increases to $\sim$ 22~MK,
  accompanied by further hardening of the spectral index to $\sim$ 3.9. Despite pronounced
  non-thermal signatures and enhanced particle acceleration, the MFR fails to erupt
  through the overlying coronal loops because of the strong strapping force exerted
  by the large overlying coronal loops. Importantly, the stalled eruption, together
  with reconnection in the higher overlying loops, results in enhanced heating within
  a closed magnetic configuration, with temperatures peaking at up to $\sim$ 15~MK
  during the late phase. The paper also provides the first observations of detectable
  non-thermal X-ray emission during the early EUV late phase, albeit briefly, providing
  direct evidence for reconnection-induced heating, likely occurring in the higher
  overlying coronal loops.
