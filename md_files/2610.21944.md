abstract: Star formation is both a multi-scale and highly dynamic process. It is regulated
  by the combination of gravity, turbulence, feedback and magnetic fields, which connect
  more than six orders of magnitude in scale... from large molecular clouds (several
  10s to 100s pc in size) down to protoplanetary disks (a few 10s to 100s of astronomical
  units in size, or 10^{-5} to 10^{-4} pc). Resolving the processes of fragmentation,
  accretion, and feedback that result in the formation of stellar systems throughout
  all relevant scales, from clouds to disks, and across the entire range of different
  environments in the Milky Way is key to constructing a comprehensive understanding
  and a predictive model of the formation process of stars of all masses that populate
  not only our Galaxy but the wider Universe across cosmic times. With current astronomical
  facilities, we are only able to grasp the details of star formation in <5% of Galactic
  star-forming regions, being heavily biased towards those close to us. Thus, characterizing
  this multi-scale phenomenon at the necessary sensitivity and resolution across all
  Galactic environments requires the next generation (sub)millimeter-wavelength facility...
  ALMA2040.
