abstract: Magnetic turbulence remains up to this day not fully understood, despite
  its important role in the behavior of plasmas. A recurring ingredient in plasma
  turbulence is the spontaneous formation and evolution of plasmoids. These represent
  magnetic islands on current sheets in two-dimensional turbulent magnetic fields
  that are consistently found in high-resolution direct numerical simulations (DNSs)
  using a magnetohydrodynamic description. However, such simulations require considerable
  amounts of computational power. Synthetic turbulence models provide an alternative,
  cheap approach to the generation of fully-developed turbulent fields. In this work,
  we first present an extension of our open-source, general synthetic turbulence BxC
  toolkit that is specifically tailored to generate 2D turbulent magnetic fields.
  We then use a novel physically motivated, but purely geometric construction that
  generates current sheet island chains, which results in fields that visually match
  the ones obtained through DNSs. We show how this construction affects the magnetic
  field lines and find a correlation with the formation of magnetic O-points and X-points.
  The magnetic flux and size of the islands in the field are found to match well with
  theoretical scaling laws. Lastly, we show BxCs capabilities by generating a 2D $N\times
  N$ turbulent magnetic field of resolution $N = 100000$ , going beyond the state-of-the-art
  reached by simulations, on a single CPU with modest computational resources. Our
  construction maintains direct control over the inertial range, and allows to make
  morphological changes to the plasmoids and fields, demonstrating plasmoid appearance,
  disappearance and merging.
