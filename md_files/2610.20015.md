abstract: Magnetic null points define the magnetic field topology in stellar coronae,
  planetary magnetospheres, and astrospheres, and are commonly associated with magnetic
  reconnection. We investigate the local structure of stationary three-dimensional
  (3D) resistive, incompressible magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) flows in the vicinity of
  an isolated magnetic null and an isolated velocity stagnation point, assuming that
  the two nulls are colocated. The governing MHD equations are solved self-consistently
  using a first-order local expansion of the magnetic and velocity fields about the
  null. For any given magnetic-null configuration, the equations admit a two-parameter
  family of stationary solutions, comprising multiple solution branches with distinct
  velocity topologies. Along these families, the velocity-null polarity can change
  at bifurcation points where the flow becomes effectively 2.5D. A single magnetic
  null can therefore support positive-radial, negative-radial, and spiral stagnation
  flows, with qualitatively different inflow, outflow, and rotational properties.
  The associated pressure and resistivity distributions are likewise determined self-consistently
  and vary across the solution family, demonstrating that they are coupled to the
  flow topology rather than prescribed independently. The generic solutions do not
  require the velocity and magnetic separatrices to coincide, allowing plasma to cross
  the magnetic fan separatrix and providing a local reconnective flow. These results
  demonstrate that, in the presence of nonuniform resistivity, the magnetic topology
  alone does not uniquely determine the associated stationary flow, pressure, or resistivity
  structure around a 3D magnetic null, revealing the nonlinear feedback between all
  macroscopic variables and resistivity. The constant-resistivity field-aligned and
  spiral field-crossing solutions identified previously are recovered as special cases.
