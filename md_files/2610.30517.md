abstract: Quantifying the rate of reconnection remains a key challenge in the study
  of geomagnetic storms. In this study, we present the first results from the new
  Tandem Reconnection and Cusp Electrodynamics Reconnaissance Satellites (TRACERS)
  mission to calculate the reconnection rate from dispersed ion populations measured
  in the cusp, using previously developed methodology. We study ion dispersion events
  in two storms. During the first storm, which occurred on September 30, 2025, 21
  dispersion events were identified in TRACERS 2 ion data as a combined result of
  geospace dynamics and the spacecraft cusp encounters. Throughout this time when
  ion dispersion was detected by TRACERS , the reconnection rate is calculated and
  is shown to track reasonably well with another measure of geomagnetic activity,
  the Cross Polar Cap Potential (CPCP) as obtained by the Super Dual Auroral Radar
  Network (SuperDARN). The dispersions are correlated with the smoothed CPCP, and
  a correlation of $r^2=0.62$ was found using the dispersions from the onset and main
  phase of the storm. The correlation disappears when including dispersions from the
  recovery period. The distribution of reconnection rates throughout this storm lies
  generally between 0.1 - 1.5 mV/m, slightly lower than previous reconnection rates
  calculated with DMSP, but consistent with in-situ observations made with Polar.
  In this work, we also apply a previously developed automatic identification algorithm
  for ion-energy cusp dispersion to TRACERS, with the intent that it may serve as
  a tool for future statistical analysis as the data volume becomes too large to manually
  review.
