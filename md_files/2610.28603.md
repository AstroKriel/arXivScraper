abstract: Solar energetic particle (SEP) events threaten space infrastructure, yet
  physics-based prediction models depend on numerous poorly constrained free parameters.
  We introduce the Fujitsu Solar Energetic particle Intelligent Tuning and Evaluation
  Network (SEITEN), which couples the SUSANOO MHD model of CME propagation to SEPIF,
  a diffusive shock acceleration and focused transport model, through the shock conditions
  SUSANOO supplies, and automatically optimizes the free parameters of the particle
  model against the SEP event of 9 October 2021. The mean free path and the injection
  efficiency vary independently along each magnetic field line and heliolongitudinal
  bin, giving a 19-dimensional problem solved by quasi-Monte Carlo sampling followed
  by a Tree-structured Parzen Estimator. The optimized model reproduces proton flux
  profiles at five spacecraft with a mean absolute error of $\approx 0.45$ in $\log_{10}$
  flux, and the inferred mean free paths show a clear east-west structure; these are
  effective parameters conditional on the assumed transport geometry. Six independent
  runs show that whether mean free paths or injection efficiencies dominate depends
  on the MHD shock strength, and that injection efficiencies differ by orders of magnitude
  between MHD configurations despite nearly identical fit quality. Joint MHD-SEP parameter
  estimation is therefore a necessary next step towards generalized, operational SEP
  forecasting.
