abstract: Weakly-collisional plasmas are found in several turbulent astrophysical
  environments, such as the Intra-galaxy-cluster medium (ICM) and the solar wind,
  where magnetic fields can be amplified by the turbulent dynamo. The onset of dynamo
  action is determined by the critical magnetic Reynolds number, ${\rm Rm}_{\rm crit}$
  , above which turbulent motions amplify magnetic energy. Although ${\rm Rm}_{\rm
  crit}$ has been studied extensively in collisional magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) turbulence,
  its value in supersonic weakly-collisional plasmas remains unknown. Here we determine
  ${\rm Rm}_{\rm crit}$ using three-dimensional hybrid particle-in-cell (HPIC) simulations
  of solenoidally driven turbulence with a sonic Mach number $\approx 2$ . We vary
  the magnetic Reynolds number over ${\rm Rm}\approx31$ - $1000$ and consider two
  initial plasma magnetisations, characterised by initial Larmor ratios $r_{\rm Larmor,
  0}/L_{\rm box}=1$ and $100$ . We find that the magnetic energy grows exponentially
  at sufficiently large ${\rm Rm}$ and decays when magnetic diffusion dominates at
  low ${\rm Rm}$ . By fitting the magnetic-energy growth rate as a function of ${\rm
  Rm}$ , we measure ${\rm Rm}_{\rm crit}=190^{+21}_{-17}$ and $198^{+16}_{-18}$ for
  the initially magnetised and unmagnetised plasma, respectively. These values are
  the same within their uncertainties, indicating that the initial plasma magnetisation
  does not significantly affect ${\rm Rm}_{\rm crit}$ in the supersonic regime. The
  measured values are higher than those previously obtained for the subsonic weakly-collisional
  dynamo, showing that increasing compressibility makes weakly-collisional turbulent
  dynamo action more difficult to excite. The measured values are also approximately
  1.5 times higher than the ${\rm Rm}_{\rm crit}$ value reported for supersonic MHD
  turbulence.
