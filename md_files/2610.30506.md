abstract: Magnetic interaction between astrophysical objects can play an important
  role and produce electromagnetic radiation in various systems, ranging from planet-moon
  and star-planet systems to binary compact objects. We extend our previous numerical
  study of an ideal magnetized plasma flowing past a conducting sphere to the case
  where the sphere is magnetized. Such a flow generates magnetic disturbances and
  produces a drag force on the sphere. We examine the drag coefficient as a function
  of the strength and orientation of the conductors internal magnetic field relative
  to the background magnetic field. We find that a strong internal magnetic field
  that is aligned with the background field significantly enhances the drag coefficient
  compared to the unmagnetized case; this enhancement is related to the increased
  magnetic flux that connects the conductor and the open, external field region. We
  also explore how the drag coefficient changes as the flow transitions from the sub-Alfvénic
  to the super-Alfvénic regime. Our results suggest that magnetic interaction may
  play a larger role in orbital evolution of some binary systems than previously expected.
