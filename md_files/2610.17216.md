abstract: Over the last decade, magnetic fields (B-fields) have become a common ingredient
  in galaxy formation simulations. Although seemingly unimportant in matching observational
  constraints such as the $M_*-M_{\rm halo}$ relations, the B-field influences the
  circumgalactic medium (CGM) gas and determines cosmic ray (CR) propagation, making
  it an indispensable non-thermal component. Nevertheless, the intricate physics of
  B-fields, particularly how they evolve to the observed $μ$ G-level field, remains
  largely elusive. In this work, we systematically address the evolution of B-fields
  over cosmic time using the FIRE-2 (Feedback In Realistic Environments) magnetohydrodynamics
  (MHD) simulations. We present results for both Milky Way (MW)-mass and dwarf galaxies,
  with and without CRs. We find that CRs have little impact on B-field properties,
  while the halo mass controls the saturated B-field strength at $z\sim 0$ . However,
  all runs reach a halo-independent magnetic-to-turbulent ratio of $f_{\rm B}\sim
  1-10\%$ , though the methods used to measure turbulence could affect this value.
  We identify a three-stage evolutionary course for the B-field in tandem with galaxy
  formation... i. expansion, ii. amplification, and iii. saturation. The feature is
  universal, though the timings depend on the initial location and star formation
  history of the gas. Flux-freezing is the predominant driver for the first and last
  stages, exhibiting power relations $B\sim ρ^{2/3}$ and $ρ^{1/2}$ , although the
  latter could also be explained by dynamical and energetic equilibria. The intermediate
  amplification stage, potentially driven by ``dynamos, is fuelled by structure formation,
  feedback, and gas turbulence. We conjecture a numerically triggered, turbulence-driven
  amplification process to explain this phenomenon.
