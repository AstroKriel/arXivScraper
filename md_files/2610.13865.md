abstract: In cosmic ray propagation through turbulent magnetic fields, transport perpendicular
  to the mean field plays an important role in various astrophysical environments.
  Numerical simulations have shown that parallel and perpendicular diffusion coefficients
  exhibit different energy scalings, causing their ratio to increase with energy,
  but a comprehensive theoretical explanation remains elusive. Here, we investigate
  perpendicular diffusion using test particle simulations in synthetic isotropic turbulence
  spanning 6-7 orders of magnitude in wavenumber, together with a theoretical extension
  of nonlinear guiding center theory that incorporates stochastic drifts. Our approach
  combines field line random walk and random guiding center drifts to describe perpendicular
  transport. We find that parallel and perpendicular diffusion coefficients exhibit
  the same energy scaling only for $r_L \lesssim 10^{-2} L_c$ , where $r_L$ is the
  particle Larmor radius and $L_c$ is the turbulence coherence length. At higher energies,
  perpendicular diffusion develops a stronger energy dependence, consistently across
  different turbulence levels. Our theoretical model suggests that this behavior arises
  from stochastic drifts induced by turbulent magnetic field components parallel to
  the mean field.
