abstract: Counter-rotating vortex reconnection is a topology-transforming event that
  leads to strong vorticity amplification and is often associated with the most irregular
  regions of the velocity field. Detecting such events directly in turbulent flows
  is a major challenge due to the ambiguity inherent in the definition of a vortex
  and the rapid proliferation of intense small scale vortices. We develop a novel
  methodology by combining two tools. The first is a local in scale, space and time
  regularity indicator that isolates spatio-temporal regions of interest. The second
  is a reconnection indicator that exploits a well-known property of vortex reconnection
  namely, that the enstrophy (volume integral of the square of vorticity) flows from
  the symmetry to the collision plane. To examine the flow of enstrophy in the correct
  reference frame, a new method inspired by orientational order parameters in liquid
  crystals is developed. We then show a proof of concept for our reconnection indicator
  by comparing it with diagnostic methods available in the literature. The method
  is validated on experimental datasets from a turbulent von Kármán flow at two different
  resolutions, one of which allows us to probe the flow below the Kolmogorov scale,
  $η$ . Our findings suggest that counter-rotating vortex reconnection could be a
  potential mechanism for the creation of motions at scales below $η$ .
