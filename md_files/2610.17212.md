abstract: Our understanding of gas torques on massive black hole binaries (MBHBs)
  relies on hydrodynamical (HD) simulations of circumbinary disks (CBDs) in which
  an $α$ -viscosity models angular momentum transport. However, CBDs around MBHBs
  near merger are hot, magnetized plasmas where transport is driven by magnetohydrodynamical
  (MHD) turbulence sustained by the magnetorotational instability (MRI). We test how
  well an $α$ -viscosity captures MRI turbulence with 3D global Athena++ simulations
  of a locally isothermal CBD around an equal-mass, circular MBHB, run in HD and MHD
  from the same initial torus on identical grids, so that only the transport mechanism
  differs. Excising the region within twice the binary separation, $r<2~a_{\rm bin}$
  , allows $1000$ orbits of evolution, several times longer than previous MHD simulations.
  Both disks deliver the same accretion rate, and the gas beyond $2~a_{\rm bin}$ exerts
  a positive torque in both, yet the MHD torque is $3.5$ times larger and $3$ times
  more variable. Near the midplane, transport is similar in the disk body but three
  times weaker in MHD at the cavity edge, where $2$ - $3$ times more mass piles up
  in a more eccentric cavity, raising the torque. The accretion rate is modulated
  mainly at twice the binary frequency in HD, but by a persistent lump at the cavity
  edge and its beat with the binary in MHD. The outer-disk torque therefore implies
  a contribution to the gas-induced phase shift in the gravitational-wave signal of
  LISA binaries $3.5$ times larger than in $α$ -disk models, and a different variability
  signature for LSST and Roman.
