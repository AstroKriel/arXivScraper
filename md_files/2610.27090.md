abstract: We investigate magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) turbulence in the DR21 molecular
  cloud using far infrared polarimetry from SOFIA HAWC+ and molecular spectroscopy
  from the Nobeyama 45~m telescope. The Velocity Channel Gradient (VChG) method is
  applied to thin velocity slices of the 12CO and 13CO J=1-0 emission to determine
  the antenna temperature gradients and their orientations in the plane of the sky.
  The relative orientations of the gradients with respect to the magnetic field position
  angles enable an evaluation of theoretically predicted alignment of elongated, turbulent
  eddies for Alfvenic, fast and slow modes and self-gravity. The projected Rayleigh
  statistic is calculated for both the full cloud and 6 regions of interest to provide
  a quantitative assessment of these alignments. Our analysis finds mostly parallel
  alignment of the gradient direction and the local magnetic field orientation in
  the Main Ridge of DR21 that comprises most of the cloud mass. While parallel alignment
  can result from fast MHD modes or cloud collapse, previous studies show spectroscopic
  evidence for the large scale collapse of the DR21 cloud, which supports self-gravity
  as the primary cause of the observed parallel alignment. One of the six sub-filaments
  that are connected to the Main Ridge exhibits mostly perpendicular alignment of
  the position angles of the gradient and magnetic field. These perpendicular relative
  orientations may be a product of Alfven and slow modes in a magnetized, sub-Alfvenic
  medium even if the sub-filament is accreting onto the Main Ridge.
