abstract: Extended diffuse radio emission in the central regions of galaxy clusters
  is commonly associated with non-thermal processes in the intracluster medium (ICM),
  but its origin and the mechanisms responsible for sustaining it remain uncertain.
  We present the discovery and multi-wavelength analysis of two diffuse radio sources,
  MKTJ0355-4237 and MKTJ0355-4230, to assess their nature and investigate their radio
  spectral and magnetic field properties through radio polarimetric analysis. We use
  deep MeerKAT L-band (1.28 GHz) observations to perform spectral analysis of the
  diffuse emission and study its polarisation properties via rotation measure synthesis.
  These are complemented by eROSITA X-ray data and DESI photometric redshift catalogues
  to characterise the host cluster environments. MKTJ0355-4237 is confirmed as cluster-scale
  minihalo with extent of $\sim 400$ kpc, while the other diffuse source MKTJ0355-4230
  remain uncertain and could be either associated with a radio minihalo or radio tailed
  galaxy. Both show steep spectra ( $α\approx -0.7$ to $-0.8$ ) that steepen toward
  the outskirts ( $α\lesssim -2.5$ ), consistent with spatial variations in electron
  ageing and re-acceleration. Polarised emission is detected in both systems, with
  fractional polarisation $\sim1\,\%$ in the centres and increasing in the outer regions
  of MKTJ0355-4237. MKTJ0355-4230 is associated with a previously uncatalogued cluster
  and shows an X-ray morphology offset from the brightest cluster galaxy, indicative
  of dynamical activity. MKTJ0355-4237 exhibits a sharply bounded internal radio deficit
  without a corresponding X-ray counterpart, within current sensitivity limits. The
  spectral steepening supports turbulent re-acceleration as the origin of the diffuse
  emission.
