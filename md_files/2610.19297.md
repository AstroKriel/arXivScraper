abstract: Using three-dimensional magnetohydrodynamic simulations accounting for ohmic
  dissipation, we investigate the long-term evolution of the infalling envelope for
  $\sim 100,000$ \,yr after protostar formation. The primary control parameter is
  the initial misalignment angle $θ_0$ between the rotation axis and the magnetic
  field, varied from $0^\circ$ to $90^\circ$ . In all misaligned models ( $θ_0 \ne
  0^\circ$ ), the pseudodisk (or infalling envelope) surrounding the circumstellar
  disk develops a highly distorted and complex morphology. Gas accretion from the
  envelope proceeds through discrete, channel-like flows, producing asymmetric streams
  rather than axisymmetric infall. Protostellar outflows are launched even in these
  complex environments, and inflow and outflow regions are strongly interwoven. Outflows
  transport angular momentum anisotropically, leading to time-dependent changes in
  disk orientation and outflow direction, which further enhance envelope asymmetry.
  We examine the relative orientation between gas flows and magnetic fields and find
  that, although statistical tendencies exist, accretion streams cannot be uniquely
  characterized as either parallel or perpendicular to the magnetic field, particularly
  in strongly misaligned cases. We also show that most infalling gas accretes at nearly
  the free-fall velocity, with only a minor fraction significantly slowed within the
  pseudodisk. Asymmetric accretion and stream-like structures persist beyond 10,000\,au,
  demonstrating that large-scale asymmetry can arise intrinsically. Our results show
  that complex streams and asymmetric envelopes naturally emerge from magnetized core
  physics, even in the absence of initial turbulence or external gas accretion.
