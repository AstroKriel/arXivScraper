abstract: The magnetic reconnection event that drives `standard` solar flares ejects
  material from the reconnection site, loading mass directly onto the newly-reconnected
  flare arcade. To et al (2024) suggested that this model can explain observations
  of the distribution of elemental abundances measured in post-flare loops... the
  reconnection exhaust directly injects plasma with coronal abundances onto the arcade,
  flowing from the apex downwards. We test this idea directly by modeling the spatiotemporal
  variation of elements with low first ionization potential (FIP), and directly inject
  material near loop apices. We show that this direct injection is capable of producing
  enhancements similar in magnitude and spatial spread across the corona to those
  observed during the gradual phase of an X11.9 flare. We also show that direct chromospheric
  heating strongly limits the width of the low-FIP enhancement to a narrow region
  near the injection site. The enhancement of low-FIP elemental abundances naturally
  explains bright knots commonly observed near the top of flare arcades.
