abstract: Ion-scale waves are believed to play an important role in heating of the
  solar corona and wind, though their generation mechanism remains unclear. Based
  on Parker Solar Probe observations, this Letter investigates the occurrence of ion-scale
  waves in the near-Sun solar wind with heliocentric distances between 0.1 and 0.2
  au. Results show that the occurrence rate of left-handed polarized waves significantly
  depends on the plasma beta ( $β$ ) and cross helicity ( $σ_c$ ). The occurrence
  rate rapidly increases with decreasing $β$ and increasing $σ_c$ . Overall, the occurrence
  rate exceeds 45 $\%$ when $β<0.2$ and $σ_c > 0.7$ are satisfied. These observations
  are consistent with the direct predictions of the helicity barrier theory that suggests
  the generation of ion cyclotron waves via imbalanced magnetized turbulence.
