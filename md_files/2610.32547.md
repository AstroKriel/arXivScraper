abstract: The chromospheric and coronal heating problem is among the major open questions
  in solar physics. Some of the possible mechanisms that can explain the rise in temperature
  that is needed to reproduce the observations require the estimation of electric
  currents. Since these depend on the spatial derivatives of the magnetic field components,
  it is necessary to obtain the full magnetic field vector from observational data
  in a 3D geometrical grid. We developed a new method to obtain the 3D geometrical
  configuration of the atmosphere using the approximation of hydrostatic equilibrium
  in three dimensions to determine the gas pressure and density as a function of optical
  depth, column mass, or geometrical distance. This development has been designed
  as an extension for any inversion code that outputs a stratification of temperature
  and gas pressure or density to enable the estimation of electric current densities
  from observations. We investigated our approach using a snapshot from a sunspot
  MHD simulation. The difference between the solver output and the simulation, taken
  as the ground truth, is mostly between 30 and 45 %, showing a similar pattern over
  a large optical depth range. Additional tests also showed that the solver provides
  a unique solution, has a scarce sensitivity to the boundary condition and is only
  marginally impacted by the viewing angle. We conclude that the electric currents
  can be inferred at a reasonable accuracy with this approach using the simulation
  parameters as input. We also tested set-ups that aim at mimicking more realistic
  cases, where the code performed equally well. The results in this work show promise
  with respect to future applications to spectropolarimetric data, however, some additional
  challenges will need to be adressed when moving from the controlled input of the
  simulation to observational data.
